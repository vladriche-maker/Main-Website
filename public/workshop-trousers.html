The problem

Most men care about the top. The bottom matters just as much.

Think about where your money goes. The jacket. The shirt. The tie. The watch. Trousers get whatever is left, usually a pair off the rack in the size that sort of fits.

But the eye doesn't work that way. Half of what a person sees when you walk into a room is below your belt. And a pair of trousers is not one decision. It's a dozen: the cloth, the rise, the seat, the thigh, the taper, the hem, the break. Get them right and the whole outfit looks like it belongs to you. Get one wrong and nobody can say why, they just know something is off.

That's why you can't wear good trousers and skimp on everything else. It's also why a great jacket can't save a bad pair. The top and the bottom have to hold each other up.

I learned this the hard way. For years I wore trousers cut for a different decade. Too short, too narrow, too tight to sit in. I kept buying new jackets to fix a problem that started at the waist.

You don't need to replace everything. You need to understand the details, so you know what you're looking at the next time you try on a pair.

That's this workshop.
